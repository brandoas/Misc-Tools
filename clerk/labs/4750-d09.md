## Task 1 - Resources and Construction Ticket

Objective: Obtain an AS number and provider-independent addresses from DRIR, order your connection through the ISP portal.  Document the findings.

1. From a browser on the gHost access the Ducky Regional Internet Registry (DRIR). Follow the prompts to request an AS number and provider-independent IPv4 and IPv6 allocations. Record your AS number and both prefixes in the network documentation.
2. Also via the gHost browse to the ISP portal. Submit the construction ticket for your connection.

## Task 2 - Connect to Your ISP

Objective: Provision connectivity to an ISP in NESTNET.

3. On your gHost, build your CPE port with `./ducky.sh cpe-up`.
4. In GNS3, place a Cloud node and connect `br-cpe1` on that cloud to the WAN interface of VyOS-1.
5. Using data sources from the ISP portal configure the VyOS-1 WAN interface.
6. Ping your ISP's router.
7. Use the first /24 and the first /64 of your allocation for a LAN that connects VyOS-1 and an Ubuntu-CLI. Address VyOS-1 on the first address of each subnet and the Ubuntu-CLI on the tenth. Set VyOS-1 as the default gateway of the Ubuntu-CLI and ensure that the Ubuntu-CLI can ping VyOS-1.
8. Reduce the MTU of the VyOS-1 WAN interface to 1450.

## Task 3 - Establish the BGP Session

Objective: Establish an eBGP session with your ISP over the CPE connection, for IPv4 and IPv6, using your AS number.

9. Create the route map that permits all routes (PERMIT-ALL).
10. Configure BGP with your AS number and the ISP as an external neighbor, one session for IPv4 and one for IPv6, on the ISP gateway addresses.
11. Display the state of the sessions and the number of prefixes received (`show bgp summary`).
12. Start a packet capture on the link between the Cloud node and VyOS-1, reset the session (`reset ip bgp *`), and identify the OPEN, KEEPALIVE and UPDATE messages.

## Task 4 - Announce Your Allocation

Objective: Announce your whole IPv4 and IPv6 allocation to your ISP and confirm the ISP is sent exactly what you intend.

13. Create a null (blackhole) route for each allocation so the router has a route to announce.
14. Announce both allocations under the BGP address families.
15. Display the routes you advertise to your ISP, for IPv4 and IPv6. Record them.
16. Display the routing table entry for your IPv4 allocation. Record the next hop and the protocol.
17. Test that VyOS-1 fragments a packet larger than 1450, using a ping with a 1472 byte payload and the Do Not Fragment bit set.

## Task 5 - Read the Routes from Your ISP

Objective: Explain what the routes from your ISP say about the path to each destination, and trace the path through your ISP to a diversity of locations.

18. Display the IPv4 and IPv6 BGP tables. Record the number of prefixes in each.
19. Display the BGP entry for 96.25.0.0/16. Record the AS path and the origin AS.
20. Display the BGP entry for 128.200.0.0/16. Record the AS path and the origin AS.
21. Display the route your router uses for 8.8.8.8. Record the protocol, the prefix and the next hop.
22. From the Ubuntu-CLI, ping and traceroute to one of the ITS DNS servers (132.235.160.10 or .11, or their IPv6 addresses), and compare and contrast the routes advertised with the direction.
23. Choose a classmate who has completed Task 4. Look up the classmate's AS number and allocation in DRIR. Display the BGP entry for the classmate's IPv4 allocation, then, from the Ubuntu-CLI, ping and run a traceroute to the tenth address of the first /24 of that allocation. Record the AS path and the output.

## Task 6 - Thought Questions

Objective: Document the final router configuration and explain the behavior observed in this lab.

24. Explain how many autonomous systems lie between you and the origin of each prefix you recorded in steps 19 and 20, and what your router knows about the other paths to them.
25. Explain what your ISP would accept if you announced a prefix outside your allocation.
26. Describe what you would change in this lab to connect to a second ISP as well as the first, and which of your recorded outputs would differ.
